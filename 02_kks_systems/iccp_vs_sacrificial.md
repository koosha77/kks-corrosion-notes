# ICCP vs. Sacrificial Anode KKS

Two families of cathodic protection (*Kathodischer Korrosionsschutz*, KKS) are used for reinforced concrete:

## 1. Impressed Current CP (ICCP / Fremdstrom-KKS)

- External DC power source drives protective current to the rebar (cathode).
- Inert anodes: MMO-coated titanium mesh, conductive coatings, discrete probes.
- Adjustable current; suitable for large structures (bridges, parking decks).
- Requires monitoring (reference electrodes, e.g. Ag/AgCl, MnO2) and periodic re-commissioning.
- Standard: DIN EN ISO 12696, Section 7.

## 2. Sacrificial Anode CP (Galvanic / Opferanoden-KKS)

- Zinc or Zn-Al anodes corrode preferentially, supplying electrons to rebar.
- No external power; low maintenance.
- Limited driving voltage (~0.25 V vs. steel); best for moderate chloride loading or as patch repair (Galvashield-type pucks).
- Lifetime governed by anode mass and current demand.

## Selection Criteria

| Criterion | ICCP | Sacrificial |
|-----------|------|-------------|
| Structure size | Large | Local / patch |
| Chloride level | High | Low–medium |
| Monitoring effort | High | Low |
| CAPEX / OPEX | High / Medium | Low / Low |
| Design life | 25–40 a | 10–20 a |

## Acceptance Criteria (DIN EN ISO 12696)

- 100 mV depolarization over max. 24 h, **or**
- Instant-off potential more negative than −720 mV vs. Ag/AgCl/0.5 M KCl, **or**
- Absolute potential decay reaching a stable passive range.
